# Quirk tracking: where we stand (2026-10-27)

All numbers: m200 Lambda 774 eV, mu = 0, test events 17000-19999, ACTS, strict matching (> 50% / > 90% purity and
completeness) unless stated. Source rows: EXPERIMENTS.md. "Coupled" = the two quirks scored as one object (pair truth).

## What we know (with the evidence)

Data and evaluation
1. The chain makes quirk datasets in the ColliderML layout at scale: 220 datasets (110 grid points x mu 0/60 plus
   m200 extensions), 7784 runs; m200_L774_mu0 has 1M events. Quirk pair production is off (Geant4 11.3 table bug;
   effect ~3e-5 of ionisation, -0.03 +- 0.03 hits per quirk). ACTS leaves the cluster direction columns at zero; an
   offline re-digitisation rebuilds them, hit-identical to production, in minutes.
2. The evaluator is reliable: Python and ACTS agree exactly on 486 evaluations; broken-truth tests behave as expected.
   Strict matching is the standard since 2026-10-26; >= (the ACTS default) credited exact 50/50 tracks and inflated
   per-quirk numbers by 6-10 points.

Finding the pair (coupled task) is largely solved at the training point
3. Pair GNN + connected components: 0.944 +- 0.003 / 0.688 (3 seeds), fake 0.079; with a quality score 0.935 at fake
   0.02. MaskFormer pair + direction, 100k steps: 0.907 / 0.553, fake 0.065 on pair truth (0.900 / 0.556 at fake 0.028).
   Ceiling with perfect scores on graph v3: 0.998 / 0.974.
4. Track building matters as much as the network: the same pair GNN scores give 0.912 / 0.118 with walk + stitch and
   0.944 / 0.688 with connected components.

Separating the two quirks
5. The per-quirk GNN v3 reaches 0.748 / 0.103 (2 seeds), far below its graph's ceiling (0.966 / 0.828): the graph is
   no longer the limit (98.1% of quirk edges kept), the edge scores are.
6. Separation is not limited by hit ambiguity: the quirks come within 3 mm of each other ~5 times per pair (median
   closest approach 0.1 mm; estimated by straight-line interpolation in time between hits ~100 mm apart, a model,
   not a measurement) but their hits on a layer are usually several mm apart; a position-only splitter that
   gets every hit pair > 1 mm apart right reaches 0.997 / 0.951.
7. "Find the pair, then split it" works, and the splitter is strongly data-limited: on exact pairs from truth (val),
   16k -> 50k -> 200k -> 971k training pairs gives 0.902 / 0.160 -> 0.918 / 0.243 -> 0.933 / 0.375 -> 0.985 / 0.824
   (16 epochs), and 0.987 / 0.859 with a larger model (d 256, 6 layers). Test on exact pairs: 0.986 / 0.865. Its
   errors at 16k were whole segments swapped between crossings; a continuity decode did not help, more data did.
8. Best per-quirk result so far: pair GNN -> quirk-tag gate -> 1M splitter (d 256, 6 layers): 0.828 / 0.505, quirk
   event 0.768, fake 0.082 (per-quirk GNN v3: 0.748 / 0.103, event 0.622, fake 0.117). The loss on real tracks comes
   from pair finding and gating, not the split: pair tracks 0.944 x gate 0.91 x oracle split 0.986 ~ 0.85.
9. Hit time adds ~1 point to the splitter; 30 ps smearing is as good as exact time.

Fakes
10. 96-98% of GNN fakes are fragments or merges of ordinary low-pT particles, not quirks. A quality score that protects
    quirk tracks takes the per-quirk GNN from fake 0.117 to 0.049 for 1 point of efficiency; an unprotected score
    removes quirks with the fakes.
11. MaskFormer builds ~75 tracks per event against ~310-320 ordinary tracks for the GNNs; its quirk tagger mistags 0.01 ordinary
    tracks per event against 0.8-1.6 for the GNNs.

Across the grid (models trained only at m200 L774, no retraining)
12. Lambda decides almost everything; mass matters little. Per-quirk works at low Lambda (quirks far apart: GNN strict
    0.98-0.99 at 100 eV) and fails at high Lambda (0.37-0.42 at 10 keV). Coupled works from ~774 eV up and fails at
    low Lambda, where one track rarely holds both quirks.
13. MaskFormer (pair) generalises worse than the pair GNN to low Lambda and slightly better to high Lambda (ahead from
    2154 eV; wins 42/110 sets).

## What we do not know yet

1. Whether the pair GNN, per-quirk GNN and MaskFormer are data-limited like the splitter (all trained on 16k events).
   The scaling studies are paused, with direction features and graphs for events 20000-153999 already built.
2. Whether the splitter and the full pipeline (pair GNN -> gate -> splitter) hold across the grid; nothing but the
   GNNs and MaskFormer has been run off the training point.
3. How to raise pair-track recall and gate efficiency, which now limit the real-track per-quirk result.
4. What can be measured about the quirks (m, Lambda) per event and per dataset. Study C1 is running; no result yet,
   including whether the toy propagator matches Geant4 well enough to fit with.
5. Anything at mu = 60: memory and speed, fakes, and whether the pair is still findable. Test sets exist (4 runs per
   grid point, 200 runs at three Lambda values for m200).
6. Strict fake rates on the grid, so grid comparisons are not yet at matched fake rate.
7. Seed spread for several headline numbers (MaskFormer long run, pair GNN on graph v3, 1M splitter: one seed each).
8. Which figure of merit an analysis needs (per-quirk, coupled, or event level); this depends on C.
9. Clean held-out numbers for the quality score (trained and tuned on val, which earlier choices also used).

## Obvious next steps (proposed order)

1. Finish C1 (running, CPU) and decide what C2 should measure.
2. Lock in the per-quirk pipeline: pair GNN -> gate -> 1M splitter, with seeds, and run it on the 110 grid test sets
   without retraining.
3. Resume data scaling, starting with the pair GNN (the remaining real-track gap is the pair tracks), then the
   per-quirk GNN and MaskFormer.
4. Train on several grid points (splitter first, cheapest), then all.
5. First mu = 60 pass: run the best pipeline on the mu = 60 test sets; measure memory, time, fakes.
6. Housekeeping: commit the code (author Daniel, no trailers), strict fake rates on the grid, seeds on single-seed rows.
